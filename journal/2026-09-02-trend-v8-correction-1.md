# 2026-09-02 — trend-v8-blend: correction 1 (sleeve averaging is a negative result)

Append-only correction to `journal/2026-09-02-trend-v8-blend-results.md`. The
original entry is not edited; this stands beside it and governs how it may be
cited.

## The correction

v8 reported that blended sleeves beat the unmodified 60/40 base with the
lowest p-values in the study (three-way blend: +0.046, p = 0.009 at w = 0.1).
That is accurate and it has been read too favourably.

**No blend reliably beat static gold.** Head to head at w = 0.1, trend+gold
minus gold alone is −0.016 (p = 0.367); gold+defensive minus gold is −0.034
(p = 0.070). Blends significantly beat the weakest components and never the
strongest one.

**Therefore sleeve averaging is a negative result as a source of edge.** Its
demonstrated value is variance reduction — the three-way blend's bootstrap SD
is 0.018 against gold's 0.038 — which is worth having when you must choose an
allocation in advance without knowing which sleeve will win. It is not
evidence that blending adds return.

## Binding on future citation

No blend weight was selected in v8, and the curve was pre-registered as
carrying no selection. **If any future entry singles out a weight — "20% is
best", or similar — that is a selection from a curve, and the trial count
supporting any claim built on it must reflect the full set of weights and
sleeve types evaluated (21 configurations).** Quoting the best point on an
unselected curve as though it were the pre-registered result is precisely the
error the trials ledger exists to prevent.
